What Abishek has built

Projects & Organisations

MenTomorrow — Founder

Co-Founder and Executive Director of MenTomorrow — a youth-led, explicitly anti-manosphere organisation built around three pillars: Representation, Research, and Community. MenTomorrow exists to reclaim and reshape modern masculinity for young men, combating online radicalisation by providing alternative frameworks of constructive, emotionally literate masculinity. Through MenTomorrow, Abishek presented research findings and policy proposals at the House of Lords, placing youth masculinity, mental health, and online radicalisation at the heart of national policy discourse — and actively pursuing legislative reform on young men's mental health infrastructure, bringing the lived experience of young people directly into the rooms where policy is made. Selected for a competitive theatre residency at Artsdepot London, collaborating with leading UK theatre-makers on a new stage production exploring masculinity and dangerous leadership inspired by Macbeth — translating research into cultural storytelling that reaches young men where policy papers cannot. Pursuing active global expansion across Europe, Asia, and international youth organisations.

Aspire Fellowship — NIH & Harvard

Out of 20,000 applicants globally, Abishek was the only recipient of a full-ride scholarship of $3,000, a 0.005% acceptance rate (1/20,000). Accepted under Dr. Shelton Lo — Postdoctoral Fellow at the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute at NIH, PhD from Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health. Conducting two landmark research studies: a geospatial analysis of institutional fragmentation in youth mental health governance across England's 151 local authorities; and a clinical pilot of Haeven — an AI-powered handheld biofeedback breathing regulation device for acute anxiety regulation in adolescents. Haeven is clinically validated by Dr. Maria Ivanov, Chair of the Royal College of Psychiatrists' Quality Network for PICUs, with a target retail price of £79–99 and a charity distribution model funding free access for those who cannot afford it.

UNICEF UK OutRight Campaign

International representative for the UNICEF UK OutRight Campaign — advocating for children's rights and international aid through youth-led campaigns linking global policy to grassroots work with 1,000+ students via The North Star Foundation. Applied the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child across three core articles: Article 28 (the right to education), Article 27 (the right to an adequate standard of living, including food and clothing), and Article 24 (the right to health and mental wellbeing) — for 300+ students in under-resourced schools. Designed and delivered mental health awareness workshops introducing foundational understanding and coping strategies to students with limited prior exposure to mental health education. The North Star Foundation's India-based community partnerships also serve as a field site for Haven's (Haeven's) clinical Study 1, linking advocacy work directly to clinical research — making the population reached through OutRight the same population generating the cross-national evidence base for scalable adolescent anxiety regulation.

NLFT CAMHS Youth Board

Worked with the UK's National Mental Health Services for Children and Adolescents — the North London Foundation Trust CAMHS Youth Board — supporting 323,000+ young people across Barnet, Enfield, and Haringey. Identified critical structural failures in the neurodevelopmental support pathway: families facing multi-year waits for diagnosis with no interim guidance, and young people falling through the gaps between referral and treatment. Led the co-production of a Neurodevelopmental Microsite acting as a central resource for families, young people, and professionals — providing clear guidance on diagnosis pathways, support services, and interim coping strategies for the years spent waiting. The microsite was shortlisted for a National Health Service Journal Award in the Empowering Patients through Digital category — a national recognition of its impact on patient empowerment and digital health equity. This work directly informs the governance fragmentation research underpinning Study 2 of Abishek's Aspire Fellowship, where the NLFT CAMHS network also serves as one of the primary field sites for Study 1's clinical pilot.

Redbridge Youth Council

Elected Youth Councillor for the London Borough of Redbridge, representing 30,000+ young constituents across the constituency. Campaigned actively against sexual misconduct, addiction, and gender inequality at both constituency and borough level — engaging peers, community leaders, local councillors, and MPs to demonstrate the tangible policy impact that youth social action can achieve. A central pillar of the council's work was the successful campaign to make the London Borough of Redbridge the first UNICEF Child-Friendly Community in the United Kingdom — a nationally significant designation recognising a local authority's commitment to implementing the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child across all its services and decisions.

#iWill Champion

Nationally recognised by the #iWill Movement — the UK's leading youth social action movement — for outstanding leadership across volunteering, campaigning, mentoring, fundraising, and community projects. As an #iWill Champion, Abishek acts as a public face of youth social action: engaging locally with peers, community leaders, councillors, and MPs to show how young people can address real priorities; and speaking openly nationally about his own journey to inspire others to take their first steps into meaningful action. The role reflects a wider conviction — not just to do the work, but to demonstrate that young people doing the work are the most compelling argument that it is possible.

Youth Ambassador — BeFreeCampaign

Youth Ambassador for Be Free Campaign — a youth-led preventative mental health charity providing evidence-based, culturally-informed support to young people aged 11–25, with backing from 10 Downing Street and the Prime Minister. Pioneering the organisation's new National Youth Network, leading its expansion from its Liverpool headquarters to London and scaling its UK-wide impact. Be Free Campaign's model — early intervention, peer-led facilitation, and lived-experience-centred design — mirrors the principles underpinning Abishek's own work across I'm Possible, MenTomorrow, and Haeven, making this ambassadorship a natural extension of his broader mission to close the gap between young people who need mental health support and those who receive it.

Global Student Ambassador — Crimson Education

Selected as a Global Student Ambassador for Crimson Education — the world's largest university admissions consulting organisation, founded by Jamie Beaton who was accepted to 25 of the world's top universities at 17. The ambassador programme connects high-achieving students globally with elite academic competitions, research opportunities, and peer networks. Abishek's role focuses on democratising access to research competitions, academic webinars, and mentoring for students who lack the institutional support to discover these opportunities independently — using his own platform and network to open doors that systemic inequality keeps closed. Outstanding ambassadors unlock internship opportunities with international competitions including Yale Model United Nations.

Associate Officer & Chapter Leader — NSRI & Synthica

Associate Officer, School Ambassador, and Chapter Leader for the National Student Research Institution (NSRI) and Synthica — international academic research organisations connecting school-age students with rigorous research competition and publication opportunities across a global cohort spanning multiple countries. Leading outreach and raising student participation in the SARC Global High School Academic Research Competition and the Synthica Research Competition — building research engagement and visibility within Queen Elizabeth's School and the broader UK academic community, and democratising access to high-quality research opportunities for students at an early stage of their academic careers.

EDI Ambassador — Queen Elizabeth's School

Equality, Diversity, and Inclusion Ambassador at Queen Elizabeth's School, Barnet — the UK's top-ranked state school. Delivered termly presentations to the school community of 1,300+ students on EDI topics including Pride, activism, and institutional inequality. Held regular meetings with fellow ambassadors to develop and implement school-wide strategies advocating for inclusive practices and policies. Raised awareness of systemic inequality and lack of diversity across one of the UK's most academically high-achieving environments — where the pressure to perform can often obscure the very real structural disadvantages that shape who reaches the room in the first place.

How the world has responded

Awards & Recognition

UN ECOSOC — New York

One of only 20 young changemakers globally invited to participate in the Transforming Global Education Summit at UN ECOSOC Headquarters in New York, 2026 — convened by PVBLIC Foundation in collaboration with the Governments of Antigua & Barbuda and the Kingdom of Tonga, alongside the Learning Economy Foundation. Invited in recognition of leadership in youth empowerment, education reform, and mental health advocacy, with organisers stating that Abishek's insights would add “strategic depth” to discussions shaping the future of global education systems. The Summit, held in the Economic and Social Council Chamber at United Nations Headquarters, was designed to bring together heads of state, ministers, multilateral leaders, technologists, educators, private sector innovators, and youth leaders under the theme “Redefining Education as Global Infrastructure.” Recognised as part of a select global cohort contributing youth-led perspectives to conversations around equitable access, systems reform, national education sovereignty, and the future architecture of global learning infrastructure.

UN Geneva — Palais des Nations

Distinguished Invitee to the Geneva Sustainable Management Workshop hosted at the Palais des Nations — the United Nations Office at Geneva — as part of the Geneva Forum's Global Impact Project to Refinance International Geneva. Invited to participate in high-level sessions exploring impact finance, global governance, strategic risk management, and scalable international development initiatives. Contributed youth-led perspectives to discussions on the Global Citizen Science Alliance for Ocean and broader sustainability frameworks addressing systemic global challenges. was invited to participate alongside international organisations, NGOs, policy experts, and governance leaders in workshops focused on credibility, stakeholder engagement, scaling mechanisms, and long-term resilience planning. Subsequently invited to continue engagement with the Geneva Forum's flagship annual United Nations programme in Geneva in recognition of ongoing contributions to international governance dialogue.

S4C & BBC News

Invited by Yvonne Alexander, founder and Executive Producer of Jet TV — an independent production company based in Cardiff — to appear as a featured contributor in a two-part current affairs documentary series for S4C, investigating how the manosphere is radicalising boys and young men and the real-world consequences for families, schools, and communities. The series, presented by Sara Manchipp (former Miss Wales and survivor of manosphere-influenced stalking), explores what makes young men vulnerable, what the appeal of toxic online masculinity actually is, and what the journey out looks like. Abishek was approached following his coverage in the Guardian and previous television appearances, with Alexander citing the honesty and clarity with which he writes and speaks about his own experiences as a powerful addition to the programme. Featured across both S4C and BBC News coverage of the series.

Jack Petchey Achievement Award

Recipient of the Jack Petchey Achievement Award — one of only 6 students selected from a cohort of 768 students across Years 10–13 at Queen Elizabeth's School, representing an approximate selection rate of just 0.8%. Awarded in recognition of outstanding contribution to the school and wider community through leadership, mentoring, mental health advocacy, and social action. Unlike conventional academic awards, the Jack Petchey Award is decided through staff nominations and peer recognition, highlighting impact, character, and service beyond examination performance alone. Directed the accompanying £300 grant entirely toward improving school mental health facilities and expanding peer mentorship support infrastructure for younger students.

Empowering Patients through Digital — HSJ National Award

Nationally shortlisted for a Health Service Journal (HSJ) Award in the 'Empowering Patients through Digital' category through work with the NLFT CAMHS Youth Board. The project focused on co-producing a Neurodevelopmental Microsite designed to streamline access to support services for families, young people, and professionals across Barnet, Enfield, and Haringey — supporting a regional population of over 323,000 young people. The microsite addressed critical structural failures within neurodevelopmental support pathways, particularly the absence of interim guidance during multi-year waits for diagnosis and treatment. Recognition by the HSJ — one of the UK's leading healthcare publications and award bodies — reflects national acknowledgement of the project's contribution to patient empowerment, accessibility, and digital health equity.

Cayley Mathematical Olympiad — UKMT

Qualified for the prestigious Cayley Mathematical Olympiad through exceptional performance in the UK Mathematics Trust Intermediate Mathematics Challenge — placing among the top 1,600 candidates from over 200,000 participants internationally, equivalent to approximately the top 0.8% globally. The Cayley Olympiad is reserved exclusively for the highest-performing mathematicians in the UKMT competition and is designed to test advanced mathematical reasoning, abstraction, proof construction, and complex problem-solving far beyond the standard school curriculum. Recognition reflects outstanding analytical ability and intellectual rigour within one of the United Kingdom's most competitive mathematical talent pipelines.

Published Author & Poet — University of Cambridge

Published author through the University of Cambridge for the elegy 'Gone' — a reflective literary work exploring grief, resilience, neurodivergence, and emotional identity. The publication examined the psychological and emotional dimensions of loss while drawing upon themes of vulnerability, selfhood, and recovery that also underpin Abishek's wider advocacy and mental health work. Recognition through a Cambridge-affiliated publication demonstrates both creative and academic excellence beyond STEM and policy-focused achievement, highlighting strong interdisciplinary communication and literary expression.

Out of 20,000 global applicants to the Aspire Fellowship, only 1,300 were admitted — and Abishek was awarded the sole full-ride scholarship of $3,000, making him 1 in 20,000. The scholarship recognises extraordinary potential at the intersection of research, innovation, and social impact, and was awarded in recognition of Abishek's work on youth mental health governance and the development of Haeven.
